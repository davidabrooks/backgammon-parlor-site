Backgammon Parlor

No cartoon boards. Real wood.

No cartoon boards — a real wood table and real light. See who’s online, challenge them, and chat. Albinoni, Mozart, Chopin, and a Living Room of solo piano.

Coming soon to the App Store.

For iPhone, iPad, and Apple TV.

Backgammon Parlor is a game of backgammon at a real table.

No cartoon boards. The board is wood, leather, suede, or stone, with real checkers and real light, sitting in one of four rooms: a mahogany parlor, a living room, a beach, or a city rooftop at night. Pinch, pan, and turn the camera, or let it follow a move and then leave the view with you. Choose one of ten board sets, or pick your own colors. The title screen flies close over the table so you can take it in.

Play the computer, play two players on one device, or play online.

Online, a lobby shows who has the app open right now, and who is already in a game. Tap Play to challenge someone; when they accept, the match starts. You can also start a game with the next available player, or invite a Game Center friend. In the lobby and during a live game you can chat (up to 140 characters). Game Center photos appear next to players in the lobby, in chat, and on the board; initials stand in when there is no photo. Report and Block are in the menus. (On Apple TV the lobby lists who is online and takes challenges, without lobby chat.)

The rules are standard: hitting and the bar, doubles, bearing off, and single, gammon, and backgammon results. There is no doubling cube; the cube stays at 1. When every remaining bear-off is forced, an Auto bear-off button can play the turn for you. Help pages on the title screen cover how to use the app, including the Siri Remote on Apple TV, and how the rules work here.

The music is recorded, not a synthesized jingle. The title screen plays Albinoni’s Oboe Concerto in D minor, Op. 9 No. 2, Adagio (Paul Arden-Taylor). A win opens with Mozart’s Eine kleine Nachtmusik; a loss, Chopin’s Prelude Op. 28 No. 7. In the Living Room, a playlist of solo piano — Satie, Chopin, and Schumann — sounds as if it is being played down the hall, mixed with quiet household noises. The other rooms have their own ambience: a fire in the Parlor, waves at the Beach, the street on City Night. You can turn the music, the room sound, or all sound off in Settings.

The same game is on iPhone, iPad, and Apple TV. There are no ads, and nothing in the game is locked behind a purchase.
